# QSSL Code of Conduct

> The community standards for participation in the QSSL project,
> including all `QSSLibrary/*` repositories, the QSSL Discord
> server, official working-group spaces, and other venues where
> people gather under the QSSL name.
>
> **Status:** Active
> **Version:** 1.0
> **Effective date:** 2026-08-08
> **Supersedes:** none
> **Amends:** none
> **Authoritative copy:** this file at the root of the

## 1. About this document

QSSL adopts the **Contributor Covenant, version 2.1** as its Code
of Conduct, as recorded in `CHARTER.md` §6. This file contains:

- **§2** — the full Contributor Covenant 2.1 text, adopted
  verbatim, with QSSL's enforcement contact filled in where the
  upstream text reserves a placeholder.
- **§3** — QSSL-specific notes on how the Code is applied in this
  project (community spaces, who enforces, how reports are handled,
  appeals, transparency).
- **§4** — cross-references to related governance documents.
- **§5** — the decision log.

The verbatim text in §2 is the **substantive** Code of Conduct.
The notes in §3 are QSSL's *application* of that text; they add
operational detail but do not modify the substantive standards.

In the event of a conflict between §2 and §3, §2 prevails.

### 2.1 Our Pledge

We as members, contributors, and leaders pledge to make
participation in our community a harassment-free experience for
everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible
disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and
expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status,
nationality, personal appearance, race, caste, color, religion, or
sexual identity and orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open,
welcoming, diverse, inclusive, and healthy community.

### 2.2 Our Standards

Examples of behavior that contributes to a positive environment
for our community include:

- Demonstrating empathy and kindness toward other people
- Being respectful of differing opinions, viewpoints, and
  experiences
- Giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback
- Accepting responsibility and apologizing to those affected by
  our mistakes, and learning from the experience
- Focusing on what is best not just for us as individuals, but
  for the overall community

Examples of unacceptable behavior include:

- The use of sexualized language or imagery, and sexual attention
  or advances of any kind
- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or
  political attacks
- Public or private harassment
- Publishing others' private information, such as a physical or
  email address, without their explicit permission
- Other conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate
  in a professional setting

### 2.3 Enforcement Responsibilities

Community leaders are responsible for clarifying and enforcing our
standards of acceptable behavior and will take appropriate and
fair corrective action in response to any behavior that they deem
inappropriate, threatening, offensive, or harmful.

Community leaders have the right and responsibility to remove,
edit, or reject comments, commits, code, wiki edits, issues, and
other contributions that are not aligned to this Code of Conduct,
and will communicate reasons for moderation decisions when
appropriate.

### 2.4 Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within all community spaces, and
also applies when an individual is officially representing the
community in public spaces. Examples of representing our community
include using an official e-mail address, posting via an official
social media account, or acting as an appointed representative at
an online or offline event.

### 2.5 Enforcement

Instances of abusive, harassing, or otherwise unacceptable
behavior may be reported to the community leaders responsible for
enforcement at the contact methods listed in §3.3 of this
document. All complaints will be reviewed and investigated
promptly and fairly.

All community leaders are obligated to respect the privacy and
security of the reporter of any incident.

### 2.6 Enforcement Guidelines

Community leaders will follow these Community Impact Guidelines
in determining the consequences for any action they deem in
violation of this Code of Conduct:

#### 2.6.1 Correction

**Community Impact**: Use of inappropriate language or other
behavior deemed unprofessional or unwelcome in the community.

**Consequence**: A private, written warning from community
leaders, providing clarity around the nature of the violation and
an explanation of why the behavior was inappropriate. A public
apology may be requested.

#### 2.6.2 Warning

**Community Impact**: A violation through a single incident or
series of actions.

**Consequence**: A warning with consequences for continued
behavior. No interaction with the people involved, including
unsolicited interaction with those enforcing the Code of Conduct,
for a specified period of time. This includes avoiding
interactions in community spaces as well as external channels
like social media. Violating these terms may lead to a temporary
or permanent ban.

#### 2.6.3 Temporary Ban

**Community Impact**: A serious violation of community standards,
including sustained inappropriate behavior.

**Consequence**: A temporary ban from any sort of interaction or
public communication with the community for a specified period of
time. No public or private interaction with the people involved,
including unsolicited interaction with those enforcing the Code
of Conduct, is allowed during this period. Violating these terms
may lead to a permanent ban.

#### 2.6.4 Permanent Ban

**Community Impact**: Demonstrating a pattern of violation of
community standards, including sustained inappropriate behavior,
harassment of an individual, or aggression toward or
disparagement of classes of individuals.

**Consequence**: A permanent ban from any sort of public
interaction within the community.

## 3. How QSSL applies this Code

This section describes how the Contributor Covenant 2.1 is
operationalized in QSSL. It does not modify the substantive
standards in §2.

### 3.1 Community spaces

The "community spaces" referenced in §2.4 include, but are not
limited to:

- The QSSL Discord server and all of its channels, including
  working-group channels and any private working-group channels
  (`governance/working-groups.md` §8.1)
- All repositories under the `QSSLibrary` GitHub organization:
  issues, pull requests, discussions, code review, commit
  comments, and any other interaction on those repositories
- Working-group GitHub Discussions areas, including WG-internal
  discussions
- Official QSSL video calls, voice meetings, and chat rooms
  (working-group calls, contributor calls, etc.)
- Official QSSL social media accounts, when posting in an
  official capacity
- Conferences, meetups, and other events where an individual is
  representing QSSL
- Direct messages between community members where the
  conversation relates to QSSL work

If you are unsure whether a particular space is a QSSL community
space, ask the project lead (Phase 1) or a TSC member (Phase 2+).

### 3.2 Who enforces

| Phase | Enforcer | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 (current) | The project lead, with co-opted reviewers as needed for impartiality | `CHARTER.md` §7, `GOVERNANCE.md` §5.2 |
| Phase 2+ | A designated TSC member, with co-opted reviewers as needed | `CHARTER.md` §7, `governance/evolution.md` |

The project lead (Phase 1) or designated TSC member (Phase 2+)
may **co-opt reviewers** from outside the immediate circle when:

- A report involves the project lead directly
- A report involves a founder representative
- The matter is sufficiently serious that a single-decision-maker
  structure is inappropriate
- The project lead or TSC determines, for any reason, that
  impartiality requires outside perspective

Co-opted reviewers are trusted contributors selected for their
judgment and lack of conflict. Their role is advisory; the project
lead or TSC retains the formal enforcement authority.

### 3.3 Reporting and contact

Reports of behavior that may violate this Code of Conduct may be
made through any of the following channels:

- **GitHub** — open a private issue on the affected repository,
  addressed to the project lead (GitHub: `@ramtinkosari`). On
  repositories with GitHub's private vulnerability reporting
  enabled, that channel may be used for sensitive reports.
- **Discord** — direct message to the project lead on the QSSL
  Discord server.
- **Email** — to a dedicated reporting address, **once one is
  established** (see §3.6).

**Current Phase 1 status.** A dedicated reporting email and a
structured private reporting channel are **not yet established**.
They will be added when the maintainer team is formed. Until then,
the most reliable channels are the GitHub private issue or a
Discord direct message to the project lead. The project lead is
required to **acknowledge a report within 7 days** of receipt,
even if only to confirm receipt and outline next steps.

**What to include in a report.** Helpful information includes:

- What happened, with as much specific detail as you are
  comfortable providing
- When and where it happened (which channel, which repository,
  which event)
- Who was involved, by name or description
- Whether you have already taken any action (e.g., asked the
  person to stop)
- What outcome you are seeking (informal conversation, formal
  warning, formal action)

You are **not required** to provide all of the above. Reports with
partial information are still investigated.

### 3.4 Confidentiality

Reports are handled confidentially. The project lead (Phase 1) or
designated TSC member (Phase 2+) is obligated to:

- Limit disclosure of the report's content to people who need to
  know to investigate or adjudicate it
- Not disclose the identity of the reporter to anyone other than
  the enforcement team without the reporter's consent, except as
  required by law
- Not publicly identify the person reported against until a
  decision has been made and an outcome is being announced

The exception to confidentiality is when disclosure is required
to protect someone from imminent harm, or when required by law.

### 3.5 How reports are processed

The processing of a report follows the pattern in `GOVERNANCE.md`
§4 and §5. Specifically:

1. **Acknowledgment.** The project lead (or designated TSC
   member) acknowledges the report within 7 days.
2. **Investigation.** The project lead (or designated TSC member)
   gathers relevant context, including input from any co-opted
   reviewers, the parties involved, and any witnesses. The
   investigation aims to establish what happened, not to assign
   blame prematurely.
3. **Outcome.** A decision is reached using the Enforcement
   Guidelines in §2.6 as the framework. The decision is
   communicated privately to the reporter and (separately) to the
   person reported against.
4. **Public summary.** A redacted public summary is published
   per §3.7.

If the matter is **urgent** (e.g., ongoing harassment, threat to
safety), the project lead or designated TSC member may make an
**urgent decision** per `GOVERNANCE.md` §4.4 — act, then explain,
then revise if needed.

### 3.6 Appeals

A person who is the subject of an enforcement decision may appeal
it. Appeals are reviewed by reviewers who were not involved in
the original decision.

**Phase 1 (current).** The project lead is recused from any
appeal of their own decision. The appeal is reviewed by **at
least one co-opted reviewer** selected for independence. If the
maintainer team is too small to provide an independent reviewer,
the appeal is escalated through the dispute-resolution procedure
in `GOVERNANCE.md` §5.3 (with the project lead recused from the
final decision) or referred to an external mediator agreed upon
by the reporter and the project lead. The bus-factor-1 risk in
Phase 1 is acknowledged in `governance/sponsorship.md` §11.1; an
appeal in Phase 1 is one of the situations where that risk is
most acute.

**Phase 2+.** The appeal is reviewed by the TSC minus any
conflicted members. The designated TSC member who made the
original decision is recused.

Appeals are decided on the record of the original decision. New
evidence may be submitted with the appeal. The appeal decision is
final within the project's governance; further escalation is via
the dispute-resolution procedures in `GOVERNANCE.md` §5.3.

### 3.7 Transparency

QSSL is **open by default**. CoC enforcement actions are no
exception:

- The existence of a report is **not** publicly announced while
  the investigation is ongoing, to protect the reporter and the
  person reported against.
- Once a decision is made, a **redacted public summary** is
  published in `decisions/` for any enforcement action that
  resulted in formal action (warning, temporary ban, permanent
  ban, or maintainer removal per `GOVERNANCE.md` §6.4). The
  summary includes the kind of violation, the enforcement action
  taken (per §2.6), and the date. Personally identifying details
  are redacted.
- Informal corrections (per §2.6.1) are typically **not**
  published, unless the project lead or TSC determines that the
  public interest warrants disclosure.
- The reporter and the person reported against each receive the
  unredacted decision privately.

The purpose of the public summary is **accountability**, not
shaming. The redaction is generous; the goal is to demonstrate
that the Code is enforced, not to publicize individual cases.

### 3.8 When this Code does not apply

This Code of Conduct does not apply to:

- Conduct in spaces that are not QSSL community spaces (per
  §3.1), even if the participants are QSSL contributors in their
  personal capacity
- Disagreement about technical, scientific, or governance
  decisions, where the disagreement is conducted respectfully and
  within the project's decision-making processes (`GOVERNANCE.md`
  §4, §5)
- Private communication that is unrelated to QSSL work

Technical and scientific disagreement is normal. It is handled
through the project's normal decision-making process — proposal,
discussion, decision, recording (`GOVERNANCE.md` §4.1). The Code
of Conduct is engaged when the disagreement becomes personal,
harassing, or otherwise violates §2.2.
